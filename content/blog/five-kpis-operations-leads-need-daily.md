# 5 KPIs operations leads should see every morning

If your morning ritual is copying numbers from sheets and chats into a deck, you are the integration layer — and that does not scale. By the time the slide is pretty, the exception that needed a decision at 07:30 has already become a customer complaint at 11:00.

These five KPIs belong on **one dashboard**, fed by the same system your team updates in real time. Not vanity traffic. Not "activity." Health of the loop: request → move → stock.

## What a morning ops KPI is (and is not)

A **morning operations KPI** is a number that changes what you do before lunch if it is wrong. If a metric only appears in a monthly pack, it is not a morning KPI.

| KPI | Question it answers | Bad signal |
|-----|---------------------|------------|
| Open requests by priority | Are we underwater? | Critical items silent >48h |
| Dispatches in transit | Where is cash and promise? | Unknown count / aging |
| Deliveries (7-day) | Is throughput real? | Unexplained dip by branch |
| Low-stock by branch | Visibility or supply? | Alerts with no inbound link |
| Approval cycle time | What is stalling flow? | Median rising; outliers hidden |

## 1. Open requests by priority

**Why:** Tells you if procurement is underwater before branches escalate in the loudest WhatsApp group.

**Healthy:** Critical and high items have owners and ages. Nothing silent over 48 hours.

**Action if red:** Triage the oldest criticals first. Do not start the day with new nice-to-haves.

## 2. Dispatches in transit

**Why:** Your cash and customer promises are on the road.

**Healthy:** You can name count, routes, and aging buckets (same-day vs overdue) without calling three people.

**Action if red:** Call the overdue bucket only. Protect the same-day lane from becoming a dumping ground for "we'll see."

## 3. Deliveries completed (rolling 7 days)

**Why:** Throughput beats anecdotes in leadership meetings.

**Healthy:** Trend up or stable. Investigate dips by branch, not with a vague global story.

**Action if red:** Compare branches that share the same SKU set. Process gap vs demand gap.

## 4. Low-stock alerts by branch

**Why:** Stockouts are almost always a **visibility** problem before they are a supply problem.

**Healthy:** Alerts are actionable — tied to incoming dispatches or open requests.

**Action if red:** An alert with no linked inbound is a ghost. Fix the link or kill the false alarm.

## 5. Approval cycle time

**Why:** Slow approvals stall everything downstream. Dispatchers cannot invent permissions.

**Healthy:** Median time visible; outliers identifiable by approver or category.

**Action if red:** Route critical categories to a backup approver when the primary is offline. Silence is a decision — a bad one.

## Framework: build the dashboard once

1. **Pick the five** — resist adding vanity until these stabilize.
2. **Bind to live objects** — request, dispatch, stock row; not pasted cells.
3. **Define healthy** — write the threshold in plain language next to the number.
4. **Assign the first action** — who moves when red?
5. **Open before email** — if a number is wrong, fix the process, not the slide.

## Mistakes that waste the morning

- Measuring "messages sent" instead of requests closed
- Averaging all branches so a failing site hides
- Updating KPIs from a Friday export on Monday
- Celebrating green charts while disputes have no audit trail
- Giving every role the same dashboard noise

## Tools

You need one database for requests, dispatches, and inventory; organization-scoped access so branches do not see each other's politics; and a mobile path so field status is not stuck on a desktop.

## FAQ

**Why only five?** Because more than five morning numbers becomes another report nobody trusts. Expand weekly, not daily.

**Should finance own these KPIs?** Finance cares about working capital. Ops owns the daily loop. Share the same underlying data; do not maintain two truths.

**How does AI help?** Anomaly detection on cycle time and stock alerts. Not a replacement for owners on critical requests.

## How to run the 12-minute morning review

1. Open the dashboard before email (two minutes).
2. Read open criticals oldest-first (three minutes).
3. Scan in-transit aging; call only overdue (three minutes).
4. Check low-stock alerts that lack an inbound link (two minutes).
5. Note approval outliers and ping backup approvers (two minutes).

If this takes forty minutes, you have too many metrics or dirty data. Fix data quality before adding charts.

### Branch comparison without blame theater

When deliveries dip at one branch, ask process questions first: Were requests approved late? Were drivers shared with another site? Was the sheet updated after the fact? Blame-first cultures drive status lies. Lies destroy KPIs faster than stockouts.

## Sample healthy thresholds (adapt to your network)

| KPI | Example healthy band | Escalate when |
|-----|----------------------|---------------|
| Open criticals | 0 unowned >24h | Any unowned >48h |
| In-transit overdue | <10% of open dispatches | Rising two days in a row |
| 7-day deliveries | Stable ±10% week-on-week | Dip with no demand story |
| Low-stock ghosts | 0 alerts without inbound | Recurring ghosts |
| Approval P90 | Inside your published SLA | P90 doubles |

Write your own bands. The point is that "red" means a human move, not a feeling.

## Teaching the team what "healthy" means

Print the five KPIs and the healthy bands next to the dispatch desk for one week. When someone asks "why is this red?", answer with the band, not with a lecture. Shared language beats another training deck.

Professionals stay relevant by learning to read operational signals the way finance reads a cash position. AI will draft summaries of these KPIs; humans still own the move when a critical request is unowned at 08:00.
